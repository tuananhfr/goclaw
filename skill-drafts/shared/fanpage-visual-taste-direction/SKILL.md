# Fanpage Visual Taste Direction

Use this skill together with:

- `fanpage-visual-creative-guidelines`
- `gpt-image-2-pro-max`
- page brand guidelines
- page visual profile
- drive reference search when real references exist

This skill is not a replacement for brand rules, route rules, or image workflow.
It is the taste layer that helps the Visual Creative Agent turn a correct idea into a strong generated image.

## Core Principle

A Facebook image should not stop at:

- correct message
- safe watermark zone
- readable text
- brand-safe route match

It must also feel visually intentional.

The goal is:

- strong first-glance impact
- clear focal hierarchy
- believable material and lighting
- controlled negative space
- enough visual energy to feel designed, not auto-generated

Taste must support the route, not compete with it.
Do not add cinematic drama when the post needs clarity and trust.
Do not force appetite logic onto non-food pages.

## What This Skill Controls

This skill helps the Visual Creative Agent decide:

- camera angle
- crop behavior
- focal priority
- lighting character
- texture emphasis
- depth and separation
- contrast rhythm
- background energy
- negative-space shape
- realism level

These choices should be decided before generating the final image so the model receives a clear visual direction instead of a vague prompt.

## What This Skill Does Not Control

This skill does not override:

- brand font rules
- watermark safe zones
- claim safety
- exact required copy
- route-specific business constraints
- asset count or output mode

Those still come from the page brand skill, page visual profile, and visual creative workflow.

## Anti-Flat Rules

Do not approve an image just because it is:

- readable
- brand-colored
- uncluttered
- technically usable

Reject the visual if it feels like:

- a safe but dead poster
- a centered object on a generic background
- a template with one headline area and no visual tension
- a food shot with weak appetite cues
- a B2B explainer that looks like a PowerPoint slide
- an architecture visual with no spatial depth
- a service visual with no believable context
- a product visual with no texture, depth, or material realism

## Visual Taste Layers

Always define these layers before generating the final image.

### 1. Focal Strength

Choose one dominant subject.

Ask:

- what should the eye hit first?
- what detail should make the image memorable?
- what gives the frame force?

The visual needs enough focal force to be noticed, but the intensity should match the page and route.
Use stronger drama only when the route supports it.
Use calmer focal clarity when the page needs trust, reassurance, or professional restraint.

Examples:

- one hero product surface
- one decisive process detail
- one strong material edge
- one human-context cue
- one spatial or structural cue

Avoid multiple weak focal points.

## Taste Intensity Control

Not every post needs the same level of visual intensity.

Choose one:

- restrained: trust-heavy, professional, healthcare, legal, education, technical, or premium-minimal posts
- balanced: most Facebook posts, service posts, explainers, B2B posts, and local business posts
- expressive: food, lifestyle, campaign, trend-led, product appetite, or community-engagement posts

Do not apply cinematic lighting, aggressive contrast, or heavy texture when the page needs calm trust.
Do not make every visual look like a campaign key visual.

### 2. Camera Intention

Choose camera angle deliberately.

Use:

- top-down when layout clarity, steps, or object spread matter
- 3/4 angle when height, depth, layering, or object volume matter
- close crop when texture or material is the main selling cue
- eye-level when human relatability, store realism, room experience, or business context matters
- wider perspective when space, workflow, or environment is part of the message

Do not default to the same angle for every route or page type.

### 3. Lighting Character

Lighting should match the communication goal.

Use:

- bright directional light for fresh, clear, practical product work
- warm controlled highlights for appetite, comfort, or hospitality
- soft diffused light for trustworthy realism and low-aggression brand tone
- crisp contrast for process, technical confidence, or architectural form
- clean neutral light when clarity matters more than mood

Avoid:

- muddy low-contrast light
- random luxury moody lighting on everyday commercial posts
- flat front light with no depth
- dramatic cinematic lighting when the page needs straightforward trust

### 4. Texture Emphasis

Decide what material cues must read clearly.

Examples:

- melted cheese
- crisp crust edge
- packaging realism
- skin or treatment texture
- fabric or material grain
- interface surface clarity
- structural material edge
- concrete, wood, glass, or metal finish

If texture is important, prompt for it directly.
Do not rely on the model to infer it.

### 5. Negative Space Design

Negative space is not just empty room for a watermark.

It should:

- protect readability
- balance the subject
- create shape in the layout
- feel intentional, not leftover

Good negative space has a clear location and purpose.

Bad negative space feels like:

- dead empty corners
- giant blank areas with no composition logic
- safe zones that make the frame collapse visually

### 6. Background Energy

Backgrounds should support, not compete.

Choose one of these levels:

- quiet: clean, minimal, subject-first
- supportive: soft props, mild texture, subtle environmental context
- active: visible motion, process atmosphere, material scatter, spatial cues

Do not use an active background when the subject is already visually busy.
Do not use a dead plain background when the image needs appetite, motion, or environmental credibility.

### 7. Contrast Rhythm

Use contrast to organize the eye.

Balance:

- bright vs dark
- warm vs cool
- sharp vs soft
- detailed vs calm
- saturated vs restrained

The frame should have visual rhythm, not one flat exposure everywhere.

### 8. Realism Threshold

Choose how real the image needs to feel.

Use:

- high realism when trust, product authenticity, technical clarity, or B2B credibility matters
- moderated stylization when the route is trend-led, social, or concept-first
- AI support only where it does not damage trust

If the page sells real products or real services, do not let the final result feel synthetic, rubbery, plastic, or fake-premium.

## Route-Based Taste Guidance

### Product Sales Visual

Should feel:

- immediate
- desirable
- fast to read
- commercially sharp

Prefer:

- one hero subject
- one memorable texture cue
- clean but not lifeless background
- one strong light direction

### Sales Explainer Visual

Should feel:

- informative
- commercially useful
- visually controlled
- structured without becoming slide-like

Prefer:

- one dominant subject zone
- one text support zone
- enough depth and realism to avoid infographic stiffness

Do not let the explanatory copy flatten the image.

### Educational Infographic

Should feel:

- organized
- credible
- easy to scan

Prefer:

- strong section contrast
- controlled icon or diagram use
- practical clarity over decoration

### News Or Insight Card

Should feel:

- topical
- editorial
- sharp

Prefer:

- decisive headline area
- one supporting visual cue
- restrained but modern contrast

### Story Or Experience Visual

Should feel:

- human
- natural
- scene-led

Prefer:

- believable context
- softer transitions
- emotional clarity without sentimentality

## Text-First Visual Taste

For quote cards, insight cards, announcements, recruitment cards, and simple educational cards, visual taste should come mainly from:

- typography hierarchy
- spacing
- contrast
- background restraint
- shape rhythm
- one supporting visual cue

Do not force camera angle, material texture, or cinematic lighting when the asset is primarily typographic.
For text-first visuals, negative space and contrast rhythm matter more than realism or camera drama.

## Page-Type Taste Notes

### Food / Beverage

When working on food:

- make heat, texture, and edibility visible
- show enough surface imperfection to feel real
- avoid sterile symmetry unless the route truly needs it
- use props sparingly and only when they improve appetite or context
- do not let props become the story

### B2B / Technical / Professional

When the page is B2B, do not confuse practical with visually weak.

B2B visuals should still feel:

- decisive
- professional
- memorable

Use business relevance through:

- context cue
- product-use cue
- process cue
- scale cue

Not through boring composition or generic AI tech effects.

### Service / Local Business

When the page sells a service:

- show believable service context
- use trust-building realism
- avoid fake stock perfection
- make the environment feel usable, not staged for no reason

### Architecture / Construction / Interior

When the page is about space, build quality, structure, or design:

- make spatial depth readable
- let material, light, and form carry part of the message
- avoid fake-premium renders with impossible geometry
- use perspective intentionally so the viewer can understand scale and use

### Education / Training

When the page teaches or recruits learners:

- use clarity before spectacle
- let diagrams, structure, or scene cues support comprehension
- avoid childish decoration unless the audience truly requires it

## GPT-Image-2 Direction Rule

This taste layer exists to improve image generation quality.

Before generating with GPT-Image-2, convert the chosen taste direction into concrete visual instructions such as:

- subject emphasis
- camera angle
- framing
- lighting
- texture or material emphasis
- background energy level
- realism level
- must-avoid list
- safe zone instruction

Do not stop at:

- product name
- route
- color
- no text

That is not enough for strong visual output.

## Prompt Compression Rule

Convert taste direction into clear, compact image instructions.

Do not overload the generation prompt with every taste layer if some layers are irrelevant.

Prioritize:

1. focal subject
2. composition and crop
3. lighting
4. texture or material cue if relevant
5. background energy
6. negative space
7. realism level
8. must-avoid list

A strong prompt is specific, not bloated.

## Final Rule

Brand-safe is not enough.
A good visual should feel deliberate, credible, and hard to mistake for a weak generic AI poster.
