# Buildtek Home Design Construction Knowledge

Use this skill when the task involves Buildtek content about home renovation, turnkey house construction, architecture, interiors, living-space planning, style comparison, practical construction tips, process explanation, or homeowner pain-point content.

Also apply:

- `facebook-fanpage-baseline-rules` for Facebook structure and baseline hygiene.
- `brand-buildtek-guidelines` for page tone, CTA, watermark, hashtags, and positioning.
- `buildtek-visual-content-profile` when image direction or image QA matters.
- `fanpage-grounded-content-rules` when the post needs believable homeowner situations or practical real-life living context.

## Role

This skill helps Buildtek communicate like a real design and construction page that understands both:

- aesthetics
- practical living and implementation

It supports content about:

- renovation
- building a private house
- architecture
- interior design
- layout thinking
- workflow and implementation steps
- practical homeowner advice

## Important Boundary

This skill supports marketing, homeowner education, inspiration, and practical guidance.
It does not replace:

- licensed structural advice
- legal advice
- project-specific cost estimation
- full technical documentation
- project-specific engineering judgment

When discussing technical, legal, structural, cost, or schedule topics:

- use cautious wording
- do not promise universal results
- do not invent cost, timeline, or technical specifications

## Core Knowledge Areas

### 1. Home Renovation

Use for:

- improving living comfort
- updating layout
- solving inconvenient space use
- refreshing old houses

Good angles:

- renovation should solve a real living problem
- layout and function matter as much as beauty
- renovation planning reduces avoidable disruption

### 2. Turnkey House Construction

Use for:

- people planning to build a house from the start
- people who need process clarity
- people comparing self-managed work vs turnkey support

Good angles:

- a clearer process helps reduce confusion
- coordination matters across design and construction
- homeowners benefit from understanding each phase before starting

### 3. Architecture

Use for:

- facade ideas
- spatial concept
- style direction
- natural light and ventilation thinking

Good angles:

- architecture is not only about appearance
- it should support lifestyle, climate, and daily use

### 4. Interiors

Use for:

- interior style direction
- room mood
- furniture planning
- storage and circulation

Good angles:

- interior choices should support how the family lives
- style should match use, not just trend

### 5. Living Function / Cong Nang

Use for:

- circulation flow
- family routines
- light
- storage
- room relationships

Good angles:

- a beautiful home still needs to be easy to live in
- function mistakes are often more painful than style mistakes

## Common Pain Points

Use these when building relatable content:

- not knowing where to start
- fear of unclear process
- fear of hidden inconvenience
- fear of cost and timeline confusion
- fear that a beautiful house may not fit daily life
- difficulty choosing a suitable style
- stress during renovation while still living in the home

## Style Comparison Rules

When comparing styles:

- explain differences clearly
- connect style to real living habits
- do not reduce comparison to surface aesthetics only

Examples:

- modern vs japandi
- minimalist vs tropical
- warm interior vs cool-toned interior
- open-plan layout vs more separated layout

## Process Content Rules

For process posts:

- keep steps clear
- explain why each step matters
- avoid pretending every project follows the exact same path
- note that real execution depends on current conditions, scope, and homeowner needs

## Tips And Mistakes Content

Good routes:

- common layout mistakes
- mistakes when renovating a lived-in house
- over-prioritizing looks over comfort
- poor storage planning
- lighting mistakes
- circulation mistakes

## Writing Rules

- explain in easy homeowner language
- keep one main idea per post
- tie aesthetics back to function
- tie function back to real living use
- keep service logic natural, not forced

## Final Self-Check

Before returning content, verify:

- the post feels relevant to real homeowners
- the advice is practical and understandable
- style content still connects to living use
- process content feels structured but not rigid
- no unsupported technical, legal, cost, or timeline claim was invented
- the content helps Buildtek look trustworthy and capable
