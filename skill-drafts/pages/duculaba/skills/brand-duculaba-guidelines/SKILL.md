# Brand Duculaba Guidelines

Use this skill for any task involving `Duculaba - The gioi do lam banh`, Vietnamese Facebook content, baking and drink-ingredient sales posts, product introductions, recipe-support content, tips, ingredient knowledge, or page-level content review under the Duculaba page.

Also apply:

- `facebook-fanpage-content-guidelines` for Facebook caption hygiene, CTA, footer, hashtag, and mobile readability.
- `duculaba-baking-supply-knowledge` for product/application logic, recipe-support content, common mistakes, storage, and safe claims.
- `duculaba-visual-content-profile` when image direction, image QA, or on-image text structure matters.
- `fanpage-grounded-content-guidelines` when the post needs believable buyer situations or practical real-life context.

## Page Basics

- Page name: `Duculaba - The gioi do lam banh`.
- Language: Vietnamese.
- Page type: sales-first page.
- Main business: selling ingredients and related products for baking, drinks, and nearby kitchen use cases.
- Watermark safe zone: top left.

Possible product groups may include:

- flour
- yeast
- butter
- cream
- milk
- cheese
- chocolate
- topping
- baking ingredients
- drink ingredients
- drink-support products
- tools or related materials only when the user confirms the page sells them

If the current product list is incomplete, do not invent exact products, brands, prices, origins, certifications, or technical specs.

## Sales Positioning

Duculaba is not a recipe page that happens to sell products.
It is a sales page that uses useful content to help customers understand products, know how to use them, and develop buying intent.

Main objectives:

- sell baking ingredients, drink ingredients, and related products
- help customers understand what the product is used for
- suggest practical applications in recipes or menu items
- build trust in product usefulness and suitability
- create inboxes, comments, product questions, and consultation actions

Content support routes such as recipes, tips, ingredient knowledge, and lifestyle should always stay connected to product understanding or buying intent.

Avoid:

- turning the page into a recipe-only page
- turning the page into a generic inspirational baking blog
- writing dry hard-sell copy with no practical value
- educational content that forgets the product or product group entirely

## Content Routes

Use the route requested by the user, content plan, or checklist when provided.
If no route is specified, choose the route that best matches the post objective.

### 1. Product Sales Posts

This is the most important route.

Goal:

- introduce the product clearly
- explain what it is used for
- explain who it fits
- suggest dishes, recipes, or drinks it can support
- create inbox or comment intent

Writing pattern:

- start from a customer need, confusion, or common problem
- introduce the product as a practical solution
- explain real application clearly
- close with a clear but natural consultation or purchase CTA

### 2. Recipe Support Posts

Recipes exist to support product sales naturally.

Goal:

- show how a product or product group can be used in a real dish or drink
- help customers imagine usage
- create demand for ingredients
- increase saves, shares, and product questions

Rules:

- do not write disconnected recipes with no product link
- each recipe should connect to at least one Duculaba product or product group
- if the exact product is not confirmed, say the product needs confirmation instead of inventing one

### 3. Tips And Troubleshooting Posts

Tips are educational, but the sales purpose is still important.

Goal:

- hit common customer mistakes
- help readers understand why they fail
- guide them toward the right product category or usage

### 4. Ingredient Knowledge Posts

Goal:

- build trust
- help customers choose correctly
- reduce buying hesitation

Use for:

- flour differences
- yeast choice
- butter vs margarine when relevant
- cream, cheese, chocolate, topping, or drink ingredient selection
- storage basics
- signs of wrong ingredient choice

### 5. Lifestyle / Soft Community Posts

Use this route only to soften the page, not to replace the sales mission.

Goal:

- keep the page warm and close
- connect products to real kitchen or shop situations
- support emotional familiarity without drifting away from product use

## Voice And Addressing

Use a tone that is:

- warm
- easy to understand
- practical
- guiding
- sales-capable without sounding pushy
- like an experienced seller who can actually advise the right ingredient

Brand feeling should be:

- useful
- trustworthy
- knowledgeable about baking and drinks
- friendly to beginners
- supportive for home bakers, small shop owners, online sellers, and drink makers

Avoid:

- overly academic language
- empty hype
- writing tips just for fun while forgetting the product
- sounding too cold or too forcefully salesy

Preferred addressing:

- `Duculaba`
- `nha Duculaba`
- `anh chi`
- `quy khach`

## Audience

Primary audiences:

- home bakers
- beginners learning baking
- online cake sellers
- small bakery owners
- drink-shop owners
- home drink makers
- people looking for ingredients, tools, or support products for baking and drinks
- people who want ingredient advice

Common pain points:

- not knowing which ingredient fits their dish
- repeated baking failures
- not knowing why a recipe fails
- unstable final results
- not knowing how to store ingredients properly
- not knowing how to use a product correctly
- wanting better results without making the process too hard
- wanting to sell cakes or drinks but not knowing which ingredients to choose

## Post Format

For normal sales-first posts:

```text
[Hook from customer need, mistake, or use case]

[Product or product-group introduction]

[Real application or buying reason]

[Natural CTA to ask, inbox, or order]

[Fixed footer]

[Hashtags]
```

Rules:

- keep the copy mobile-readable
- use short paragraphs
- make the product use case concrete
- connect useful content back to product understanding or product demand
- do not show labels like AIDA, PAS, Hook, Problem, or CTA in publish-ready copy

## CTA Guidance

Soft CTA examples:

- `Luu lai de khi lam banh can thi xem nhe.`
- `Ban muon Duculaba chia se cong thuc mon nao tiep theo?`
- `Comment mon ban hay bi loi, Duculaba goi y cach xu ly.`

Sales CTA examples:

- `Inbox Duculaba neu ban can tu van san pham phu hop voi cong thuc nay.`
- `Can bot/men/bo/kem/topping cho mon nay? Nhan Duculaba de duoc tu van.`
- `Comment "NGUYEN LIEU" de duoc goi y san pham phu hop.`
- `Muon dat san pham, inbox Duculaba de duoc tu van.`

If price is not provided, ask customers to inbox for the current price instead of inventing one.

## Claim Safety

Do not invent:

- price
- discounts
- ingredients list
- origin
- certification
- special effects
- technical specs
- shelf life
- exact storage instructions without data
- guaranteed success
- customer feedback
- before/after claims without evidence

Be careful with:

- `tot nhat`
- `ngon nhat`
- `re nhat`
- `an toan tuyet doi`
- `dung la chac chan thanh cong`
- revenue or profit implication for small shops

Safer wording:

- `co the giup thao tac de hon neu dung dung cach`
- `phu hop cho nhung ai muon tiet kiem thoi gian o buoc...`
- `ban nen thu tren me nho truoc de dieu chinh theo lo va khau vi`
- `thanh pham con phu thuoc vao cong thuc, nhiet do, thoi gian, nguyen lieu, va cach bao quan`

## Final Self-Check

Before returning Duculaba content, verify:

- the post still behaves like a sales-first page
- useful content supports product understanding or demand
- the product or product group is clear
- the CTA suits the route
- the fixed footer is present unless intentionally omitted by the user
- hashtags start with `#duculaba` when used
- the top-left watermark safe zone remains clear in image briefs
- no unsupported claim is introduced
