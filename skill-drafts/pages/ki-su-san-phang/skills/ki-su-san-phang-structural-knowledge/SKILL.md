## Important Technical Boundary

This skill supports general education and communication. It does not replace certified structural design, code compliance review, site supervision, legal advice, or official professional judgment.

When discussing standards, structural systems, load, span, punching shear, deflection, vibration, seismic/wind design, fire safety, material performance, ESG, cost, or approval:

- Use cautious wording.
- State that project-specific design must be checked by qualified engineers and applicable local standards.
- Do not claim a system is always better, always cheaper, always safe, or always compliant.
- Do not invent numerical savings, span capacity, slab thickness, load capacity, CO2 reduction, or standard clauses.
- Include source notes when referencing specific standards, regulations, statistics, or current technical claims.

Avoid phrases equivalent to:

- "chắc chắn tiết kiệm"
- "luôn an toàn"
- "đạt chuẩn trong mọi trường hợp"
- "không cần kiểm tra"
- "giải pháp tốt nhất cho mọi công trình"

## Core Knowledge Areas

### Construction Standards And Vietnam Comparison

Use for posts comparing international construction standards with Vietnamese practice.

Possible angles:

- How Eurocode, ACI, BS, or other standards approach a structural topic differently.
- Why direct comparison between standards must consider load combinations, material assumptions, detailing rules, safety factors, and national annexes.
- What Vietnamese engineers should verify before applying an international reference.
- How standards influence flat slab checks, punching shear, deflection, vibration, reinforcement detailing, durability, and construction documentation.

Caution:

- Do not cite exact clauses without source verification.
- Do not present a foreign standard as automatically valid for Vietnam.
- Distinguish "reference for learning" from "basis for approval."

### Flat Slab / Sàn Phẳng Không Dầm

Use for posts about flat slab systems, beamless slabs, architectural flexibility, MEP coordination, and construction workflow.

Possible angles:

- When flat slab helps create cleaner ceiling zones.
- Coordination benefits and risks for MEP, architecture, and structure.
- Design checks that should not be ignored: punching shear, deflection, vibration, openings, column layout, construction sequence, and reinforcement congestion.
- Why early structural coordination matters.

Caution:

- Do not overclaim span, cost, slab depth, or constructability.

### UBoot Beton / Hollow Slab

Use for UBoot Beton, hollow slab, void-forming modules, and material-efficiency content.

Possible angles:

- Explain how void-forming systems reduce unnecessary concrete volume in selected slab zones.
- Discuss relationship with long spans, reduced self-weight, material efficiency, and design coordination.
- Explain why module layout, reinforcement, punching zones, openings, and construction quality matter.

Caution:

- Do not claim fixed savings or performance improvements without project data.
- Do not imply UBoot is suitable for every slab.
- Structural verification remains mandatory.

### Long-Span Slab / Sàn Vượt Nhịp

Use for posts about large spaces, fewer beams, parking, commercial floors, villas, public buildings, or irregular floor plans.

Possible angles:

- Long span is not only an architecture question; it affects structure, deflection, vibration, MEP, construction method, and cost.
- Early comparison between traditional beams, flat slab, prestressed slab, steel/composite solutions, or voided slab can reduce redesign.
- Documentation and model coordination help teams see risks earlier.

Caution:

- Feasibility depends on project-specific geometry, loads, materials, and design standards.

### Complex Structural Projects

Use for posts about transfer floors, basements, high-rise buildings, irregular geometry, large openings, heavy equipment, retrofit, or difficult coordination.

Possible angles:

- Complex structures fail in coordination before they fail on paper.
- Drawing/model quality affects site execution.
- Key questions before design: load path, column grid, openings, construction stages, site constraints, MEP conflicts, and responsibility matrix.

Caution:

- Avoid turning anonymous experience into claims about identifiable projects unless permission exists.

### Green Materials / ESG

Use for posts about material efficiency, green construction, ESG, lifecycle thinking, and responsible construction claims.

Possible angles:

- Material efficiency is one part of sustainability, not the whole ESG story.
- Reducing concrete volume may support environmental goals only when measured against actual project design and lifecycle assumptions.
- BIM/documentation can support transparency, quantities, and decision tracking.

Caution:

- Do not claim ESG compliance, carbon reduction, green certification, or environmental superiority without data and source.

## Writing Rules

- Explain one technical concept per post.
- Start from a real engineering question, misconception, or site/design situation.
- Use practical Vietnamese terms first; include English terms in parentheses only when helpful.
- Keep normal posts concise and mobile-readable.
- Use icon bullets only when they improve scanability.
- Add a caution line for standards, structural safety, or project-specific design.
- If comparing standards, state the comparison is for learning and must be checked against applicable project requirements.
- Include sources when using specific code clauses, official standards, current regulations, statistics, or legal/compliance claims.

## Recommended Post Structure

Use this structure for technical Facebook posts:

1. Title: short, direct, and technical.
2. Opening: name the engineering question or misconception.
3. Explanation: 2-4 short paragraphs or a compact bullet list.
4. Practical takeaway: what engineers/project owners should check.
5. Caution: mention project-specific verification when needed.
6. Soft CTA: invite discussion or technical consultation.
7. Footer and hashtags from the brand skill.

Do not show labels like "Problem", "Solution", "AIDA", or "PAS" in publish-ready copy unless the user asks for a breakdown.

## Image Brief Guidance

For design tasks, request images that show:

- Flat slab diagram, UBoot module concept, reinforcement detail, section cut, structural model, drawing checklist, standard comparison table, or construction site detail.
- Clean technical layout with enough negative space for a short headline.
- Top-left watermark safe zone reserved.
- No confidential project information or unrelated logos.

For highly technical diagrams, prioritize clarity over visual drama.
