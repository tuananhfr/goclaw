# Sỉ Pizza nướng lửa Hip'S Visual Content Profile

Use this skill as the page-specific visual profile for `Sỉ Pizza nướng lửa Hip'S`.

This is mainly used by:

- `Page Manager`
- `Visual Creative Agent`
- `Content Writer Agent`
- `Sales Angle Agent`
- `Brand QA / Claim Safety Agent`

Combine this skill with:

- `fanpage-visual-creative-guidelines`
- `fanpage-visual-taste-direction`
- `brand-si-pizza-nuong-lua-hips-guidelines`

The Page Manager owns this profile and passes the relevant part into each task packet.
This is a default map for the page, not a hard lock for every future post.

## Core Principle

This page should not look like:

- a consumer pizza ad page only
- a generic franchise page
- a heavy infographic board every time
- a single-color social template with one line of text and one CTA only

It should look like a B2B food-sales page that understands products, production quality, and store operations.

## Global Visual Direction

- tone: practical, appetizing, commercial, B2B-friendly
- keep food appealing, but connect the image to selling and operating context
- taste direction: product-real, decisive, useful-looking, operationally relevant
- avoid random luxury ad aesthetics
- avoid overly empty hero posters when the route needs explanation
- avoid dense report-style infographic layouts unless the brief explicitly asks for educational infographic treatment
- avoid one-color flat backgrounds with one line of text and no visual depth
- avoid layouts where the CTA is larger or louder than the product or business message
- avoid relying on one generic text block to carry the entire image

Shared page taste defaults:

- the image should carry one strong food or process cue and one clear business-fit cue
- practical does not mean visually weak
- lighting should help trust and appetite at the same time
- negative space should protect text and watermark without draining the frame
- color should come from food, heat, surface, packaging, kitchen context, or business environment, not from a blank filler background

## Page-Specific Anti-Weak Visual Rules

Reject the visual if it looks like:

- a single-color social template with one headline and one CTA only
- a text poster that could fit any pizza page by swapping the logo
- a product post with no visible product texture
- an operations post with no visible store-fit cue
- a process post with no real process detail

For this page, the image should usually prove something visually:

- what the product looks like
- what the process looks like
- what the store-fit situation looks like
- or why the buyer should care

## Exact Asset Requirement

If the visual depends on an exact product, packaging, logo, menu, store setup, production space, or real process proof, use the provided reference asset.

If no asset is provided:

- do not invent exact packaging
- do not invent logo placement
- do not invent real production environment
- do not create fake proof-like process scenes
- use a clearly generic or placeholder visual direction, or ask for the asset when accuracy is required

For product-led posts, real product references are strongly preferred.

## Visual Route Mapping

### 1. Product Line Posts

Use visual mode:

- `Product Sales Visual`

Style:

- product-led
- appetizing
- commercial
- clear enough for wholesale buyers

Taste direction:

- let the food look hot, sellable, and menu-ready
- add just enough business relevance through setting, crop, or support prop
- use surface, tray, box, oven, counter, menu, or serving cues when they strengthen B2B usefulness
- avoid a dead catalog look and avoid a consumer-only beauty shot

Text density:

- low to medium

On-image text default:

- 1 strong headline
- optionally 1 short support line
- optionally 1 short CTA

Important:

- CTA is secondary, not the hero
- if the image only has product plus big CTA text, treat it as too weak for this page unless the brief explicitly asks for a simple promo card

Subject focus:

- pizza dòng Ý
- pizza dòng Việt
- mỳ Ý
- combo or menu-fit context

Avoid:

- pure beauty shot with no business relevance when the route is B2B
- too much text covering food

Recommended camera cues:

- top-down for menu spread or topping clarity
- 3/4 for crust, thickness, heat, and ready-to-sell realism
- use one appetite trigger that feels immediate at feed size

### 2. Production Process And Quality Posts

Use visual mode:

- `Sales Explainer Visual`
- or `News Or Insight Card` when the topic is more process-fact than sales-led

Style:

- process credibility
- clarity
- handmade or production realism
- commercial trust

Taste direction:

- show one decisive process detail instead of a vague kitchen mood
- use realistic texture and controlled contrast
- make the frame feel hands-on, not industrial or sterile
- let dough, crust, heat, tray, handwork, or prep surface create visual richness instead of using a plain branded background

Text density:

- medium

On-image text default:

- 1 headline
- 1 short setup line when needed
- 1-4 short support points depending on asset size, brief, and readability

Subject focus:

- dough
- crust
- fire-baked process
- handmade steps
- preparation or production environment

Avoid:

- turning the image into a factory compliance chart
- using only one vague headline with no explanation when the route is process explainer

Recommended visual construction:

- one process hero cue
- one support text zone
- one quiet negative-space zone for watermark and breathing room

### 3. Store Operations And Experience Posts

Use visual mode:

- `Sales Explainer Visual`

Style:

- practical
- buyer-problem aware
- menu-expansion logic
- bếp nhỏ or store-fit context

Taste direction:

- show compact-store practicality through framing, not only copy
- one visible business-fit cue should be obvious even before reading the text
- use counter, oven, tray, takeaway setup, drink-shop add-on context, or compact-prep cues when relevant
- avoid flat poster layouts with text stacked over generic food

Text density:

- medium

On-image text default:

- 1 headline
- 1 short framing line if needed
- 1-4 short support points depending on asset size, brief, and readability
- optional 1 short CTA or closing line

Subject focus:

- small kitchen
- food counter
- drink shop menu expansion
- frozen pizza operating simplicity

Avoid:

- looking like a franchise ad if the page is discussing wholesale product deployment
- looking like a generic food poster with no operations angle

Recommended camera cues:

- slightly elevated or 3/4 angle so counter, tray, oven, or compact setup logic can read
- avoid perfectly centered dead symmetry unless the brief explicitly needs it

### 4. Market And Buyer Context Posts

Use visual mode:

- `News Or Insight Card`
- or a light `Sales Explainer Visual` when the post interprets market context for buyers

Style:

- market-aware
- editorial but commercial
- clean hierarchy

Taste direction:

- keep one strong news cue and one business-context cue
- contrast should feel sharp and current, not noisy
- avoid generic business stock scenes that break food-page credibility

Text density:

- medium

On-image text default:

- 1 headline
- 1 short subhead or 1-2 short support points

Important:

- the editorial cue should lead
- the CTA, if any, should stay light and secondary

Avoid:

- empty hero poster
- overly dramatic stock-business visuals

## Text Density Defaults

- Low: appetite-led product, quick promo, simple menu support
- Medium: product difference, process trust, operations explainers, buyer context
- High: use only when the Manager explicitly wants infographic treatment

These are defaults. The Manager may override them for the current post.

## Series Consistency Rules

For recurring post series, keep enough consistency for recognition but vary the layout by route.

Keep consistent when provided:

- logo or watermark behavior
- headline area logic
- color temperature
- product photography direction
- spacing and text hierarchy

Vary by route:

- product posts should prioritize appetite and product clarity
- process posts should prioritize process detail and trust
- operations posts should prioritize store-fit context
- market posts should prioritize editorial clarity

Do not make every series post use the same one-line poster layout.

## Default Pass Expectations

### Product Sales Visual

- should have at least 1 clear headline
- may stay minimal if the product visual already communicates strongly

### Sales Explainer Visual

- should normally have at least 1 headline and an explanatory support layer
- the support layer may be 1-4 short points depending on the brief, asset size, and readability
- may add 1 setup line and 1 short closing line when useful
- if the image is only a mood shot with one headline and no practical explanation, treat it as too weak unless the Manager explicitly requested hero-poster mode

## CTA Treatment On Image

- product posts may use a light CTA
- store-operations or process explainers may use a short CTA if lead intent matters
- keep long CTA copy in the caption, not on the image
- do not let CTA become the largest or most visually dominant element
- if forced to choose, prioritize product clarity or business-fit clarity over CTA size

## On-Image Text Value Rule

For this page, on-image text is not only for attention.
It should help the buyer understand quickly:

- what is convenient
- how fast the product can be served
- what process cue matters
- what menu-fit or store-fit situation it supports

Avoid emotional, empty, or generic headlines if they do not add information value.
Do not use on-image text that only repeats the caption hook without making the visual more useful.

## Facebook Feed Practical Rules

Design for mobile feed scanning.

- The product, process, or business-fit cue should be understandable within 1-2 seconds.
- Avoid small text over food texture.
- Keep the main subject and headline away from crop-sensitive edges.
- Use square or 4:5 composition when size is not specified.
- If the post needs multiple points, make each point short enough to read at feed size.

## Quality Filters

Before approving a visual, verify:

- the image matches the current route
- the product or business context is obvious
- food still looks appetizing where relevant
- the text amount fits the route
- the image supports a B2B wholesale page, not just a consumer pizza ad
- the visual does not look generic or over-decorated
- the frame is not just a one-color background with floating text
- one focal cue is strong enough to be remembered at feed size
- lighting and texture help trust instead of flattening the frame
- business-fit context is visible, not only implied by text

## Final Rule

For this page, a good image should make buyers understand not just the food, but why the product fits a real store or menu situation.
